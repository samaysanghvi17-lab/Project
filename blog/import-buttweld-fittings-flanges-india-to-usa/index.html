Importing Buttweld Fittings and Flanges from India to the USA

A checklist for US importers: documents, marking, HTS codes, importer security filing, duties and Buy America checks when buying fittings and flanges from India.

Buying fittings and flanges direct from an Indian manufacturer can cut cost and give you access to special sizes and alloys. A good import goes smoothly when you have the paperwork, marking and duty position settled before you order. This is a general checklist, not legal or customs advice.

1. Check the duty position first

US steel tariffs and anti-dumping or countervailing duty orders change often. Before you order, ask your customs broker to confirm the current position for your exact product and country of origin.

2. Classify the goods

Pipe fittings and flanges of iron or steel fall under Harmonized Tariff Schedule heading 7307. Butt welding fittings and flanges are separate subheadings, with separate lines for stainless and other steel. Your broker should confirm the exact code.

3. Gather the documents

Typical documents are the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificates. Ocean cargo also needs an Importer Security Filing, usually made by your broker, before the goods are loaded.

4. Plan the marking

US customs requires permanent country-of-origin marking on imported goods. Fittings are also marked to MSS SP-25 with grade, size, schedule and heat number. Agree both with your supplier.

5. Check project rules

Projects funded with federal or state water and infrastructure money can carry Buy America or American Iron and Steel requirements that imported material may not satisfy. Confirm this before you source.

6. Choose the delivery term

FOB and CIF leave customs clearance and inland delivery to you. DAP or DDP puts more of the logistics on the seller. Compare quotes on landed cost, not just the factory price.
