Weld Neck vs Slip-On Flanges: How to Choose

Compare weld neck and slip-on flanges on strength, cost, welding and typical use, so you can specify the right flange for your piping.

By Neo Impex Stainless Pvt. Ltd. · Published 2026-11-22

Weld neck and slip-on flanges both bolt to the same ASME B16.5 pattern, so they connect to each other. They differ in how they are attached to the pipe, and that affects strength, cost and inspection.

Weld neck flanges

A weld neck flange has a long tapered hub that is butt-welded to the pipe. The hub carries stress smoothly into the pipe, and the butt weld can be radiographed. This makes weld neck the usual choice for high pressure, high temperature, cyclic and critical service.

Slip-on flanges

A slip-on flange slides over the pipe and is fillet-welded inside and outside. It costs less, needs less precise cutting and is easier to align. It is lower in strength and fatigue life than a weld neck flange, so it suits moderate pressure and non-critical lines.

Quick comparison

Strength and fatigue: weld neck is better. Cost: slip-on is lower. Alignment: slip-on is easier. Inspection: weld neck butt weld can be radiographed. Typical use: weld neck for critical and high-pressure lines, slip-on for utility and low-pressure lines.

What to specify

For either flange, state size, pressure class, facing (raised face, RTJ), material and standard. For weld neck flanges also state the pipe schedule so the bore is machined to match.
