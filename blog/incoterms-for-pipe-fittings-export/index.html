Incoterms for Pipe Fittings Export: FOB, CIF, DAP and DDP

What FOB, CIF, DAP and DDP mean when you buy buttweld fittings and flanges from India, who pays for what, and how to compare quotes on landed cost.

Incoterms say who pays for freight, insurance, customs and risk at each step of an export. When you compare quotes for buttweld fittings and flanges, the term decides whether two prices are really comparable.

FOB: free on board

The seller delivers the goods on board the ship at the Indian port. You pay the ocean freight, insurance, import customs and delivery from the port of arrival. FOB gives the buyer most control and the lowest quoted price.

CIF: cost, insurance and freight

The seller pays freight and minimum insurance to your destination port. You pay import customs and inland delivery. The price is higher than FOB because it includes freight.

DAP: delivered at place

The seller delivers to the named place, for example your warehouse, ready for unloading, and pays freight to there. You pay import customs and duties.

DDP: delivered duty paid

The seller handles delivery and import clearance and pays duties. It is the simplest for the buyer but needs the seller to act as importer, so it is used less often for steel products.

Comparing quotes

Convert every quote to landed cost: price, freight, insurance, duty and delivery. A low FOB price can cost more than a DAP quote once freight and fees are added. Tell us your delivery port or ZIP code and we will quote on your terms.
