Flange Pressure Classes: Class 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500 and 2500

What ASME B16.5 flange classes mean, why a class number is not a pressure in psi, and how material and temperature change the allowed pressure.

By Neo Impex Stainless Pvt. Ltd. · Published 2026-11-23

A flange class such as 150 or 300 is a rating category, not a pressure in psi. The pressure a flange can hold depends on the class, the material and the temperature.

The classes

ASME B16.5 covers Class 150, 300, 400, 600, 900, 1500 and 2500. Higher classes have thicker flanges and larger bolting, so they hold more pressure.

Why class is not psi

The allowed working pressure falls as temperature rises and differs between material groups. As an example, for carbon steel A105 at ambient temperature (up to about 38 °C or 100 °F), B16.5 gives roughly 285 psig for Class 150, 740 for Class 300, 1480 for Class 600, 2220 for Class 900, 3705 for Class 1500 and 6170 for Class 2500. At higher temperatures the values are lower.

How to choose

Take the design pressure and temperature of the line, find the material group of your flange in the ASME B16.5 pressure-temperature tables, and pick the lowest class that covers both. Your line class specification normally does this for you.

What to put on the order

State size, class, facing, flange type, material grade and standard, for example: 4″ Class 300 WNRF, SCH 80, ASTM A105, ASME B16.5. The figures above are for orientation only; always use the current standard for design.
